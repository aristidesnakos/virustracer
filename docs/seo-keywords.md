# SEO keywords: what people type, and what each page should say

Written 2026-11-10. Update it when titles change or the autocomplete harvest is re-run, and date the change.

## How this was done, and what it can and cannot tell us

- **Method:** the `keyword-family-gate` skill's step 1, Google autocomplete, for 118 queries across our diseases and the general "outbreak tracker" family (`scripts/probe-keywords.mjs`, run from a GitHub runner by the manual "Probe keywords" workflow; the cloud sandbox cannot reach Google). US English, 2026-11-10, 0 errors.
- **What autocomplete shows:** which phrasings people really type, and the order Google offers them. **It is not search volume.** A query that returns nothing may still be searched a little; one that returns ten completions is searched a lot more than one that returns two, but we cannot say by how much.
- **Gates applied (run 2, 153 queries, 2026-11-10):** G1 and G3 were computed on every completion (results below); G2 and the volume and bid columns need Keyword Planner.
- **Not done:** Google Keyword Planner (step 3 of the skill). It needs a signed-in Google Ads account in a browser, which a cloud session does not have. Volumes and bids below are therefore **unknown**; the paste-ready list at the end is for whoever has the account. The skill's pass/fail gates (free-attached, paid bidding, paid intent) are built for commercial keywords: they are reported below, but they trim every informational term, so they say nothing about whether a page should target a keyword organically.
- **Competitor notes** come from a general web search, not from Google's ranking. They show who publishes on each topic, not who ranks first. Check Google Search Console for our own impressions and positions; nothing here replaces it.
- **The `keywords` meta tag does not rank.** Google ignores it. The fields that matter are the `<title>`, the meta description, the H1 and other headings, and the visible copy; the `keywords` arrays in `src/data/outbreaks/*.ts` are cheap to keep tidy but worth almost nothing.

- **G1 passes everywhere** (no completion contains free, open source or no cost).
- **G2 (bids) and the volume, bid and competition columns are unknown** until Keyword Planner is read in a signed-in browser; the 7-point score cannot be completed without them.
- **Egg and poultry:** searchers type "bird flu egg prices", "egg prices forecast", "egg price tracker", "egg futures" and (in India) wholesale egg rates by city. Nothing in the phrasing of the planned product (disease alerts, a feed, a subscription: "poultry disease alert service", "egg price risk avian flu", "h5n1 poultry outbreak map", "egg market data subscription") returned any completion. That does not prove there is no business demand, since buyers may not search this way, but autocomplete gives the product category no support. A web search for the lead term returned syndicated 2025 articles and a slide deck (thin and out of date), and the free official route (USDA ERS and AMS) fully serves price data.
- **Results-page notes** (a general web search, not Google rankings; no result in any family sells a service): Ebola and US measles are crowded with official bodies and major news (WHO AFRO, CDC, Johns Hopkins); Candida auris and West Nile Greece show no dedicated tracker; the all-disease family has apps and aggregators but no authoritative incumbent; the hantavirus timeline is covered by syndicated AP pieces.

## Recommendations

1. **Change titles on the pages above, and keep the claim honest.** Avoid "today" and "live" in titles: our figures are daily or weekly and carry an as-of date (the Ebola figures sat frozen for a week in November). "Tracker", "map", "death toll", "cases by state" and the year are the high-evidence words.
2. **Meta descriptions already lead with the numbers**, which is right for "death toll" queries. Add the literal phrase a searcher types: "Ebola death toll: N deaths…", "US measles cases by state…", "West Nile virus in Attica…".
3. **Put the query words in visible headings**, not only the meta tags: for example an H2 "Ebola death toll and confirmed cases" above the stat strip, "Candida auris cases by state" above the table, and a short, honest line answering "candida auris deaths" (the CDC table does not report deaths). A short visible Q&A helps readers; do not expect an FAQ rich result, because Google has limited those to authoritative government and health sites.
4. **Don't oversell the nursing-home pages.** There is no autocomplete evidence of demand in the phrasing we use. Keep them accurate; do not rewrite titles for volume we cannot see.
5. **Opportunities seen, not acted on:** "candida auris map" has no interactive 2026 map in the results we saw; "hantavirus outbreak tracker" interest is high while our record is archived; Kenya appears in many Ebola completions ("ebola outbreak 2026 kenya", "ebola cases in kenya"), but cover it only if the page's own data supports it (our feed has unverified signals, not confirmed cases).
6. **Check Search Console** for the queries and pages we already get impressions for, and compare with this list before changing a title that is working.
